# Architecture

## How it works

`scripts/run_calls.py` starts a LiveKit Agents worker and, for each scenario, creates a LiveKit room and dispatches the `patient-caller` agent into it, passing the scenario id in the job metadata. The agent starts its voice session first, then asks LiveKit SIP to dial the clinic through a Twilio Elastic SIP trunk (`CreateSIPParticipant` with `wait_until_answered`). When the call connects, the phone line is just another participant in the room. The bot then runs a classic pipeline: Silero VAD and LiveKit's semantic turn detector decide when the clinic has finished a turn; Deepgram Nova-3 transcribes it; GPT-4.1-mini, prompted as the scenario's patient, writes a short spoken reply; and Cartesia Sonic-3 voices it back down the line. The bot lets the clinic greet first, steers toward its scenario goal, uses a `hold_silently` tool when the receptionist says "one moment", and calls `end_call` after saying goodbye. A watchdog wraps up overlong calls. Every conversation turn, with its start time and our end-to-end latency, is logged. LiveKit's session recorder writes both sides onto one timeline as stereo OGG, which is exported along with a mono MP3, so `[01:23]` in a transcript is 1:23 in the audio. Afterwards, `scripts/analyze_calls.py` runs an LLM QA pass over each transcript against the scenario's "expected behaviour" to surface candidate bugs, which I then confirm by listening.

The design is driven by the first thing a reviewer does: listen to the calls. That made turn-taking the central problem. A caller that jumps into the receptionist's "let me check… okay, I have Tuesday at" pause sounds like a bot and derails the call. So I used a semantic end-of-turn model on top of VAD, with a minimum endpointing delay of about 0.7 s rather than the 0.5 s default. That trades roughly 200 ms of response latency for far fewer talk-overs, because a patient who pauses slightly before answering sounds normal. I also gave the LLM an explicit way to stay silent, and kept persona turns to one or two sentences. Latency is kept low elsewhere instead. Every stage streams. Preemptive generation starts the LLM on the interim transcript. The LLM is a small fast model, since patient lines need little reasoning. Cartesia was chosen for its time-to-first-audio (roughly 100 ms) and natural prosody on 8 kHz phone audio. Deepgram was chosen for low-latency streaming and good accuracy on telephony audio, with smart formatting so dates and phone numbers come back readable. Scenarios are data (YAML), not code: each gives the bot a goal and a few pressure tests to work in naturally, rather than a script. That keeps calls sounding like a real person while still reliably steering into the edge cases (closed days, controlled-substance refills, made-up insurance plans, emergencies, third-party privacy requests, prompt injection, barge-in).

## Key decisions and alternatives

| Decision | Chosen | Alternatives considered | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone connectivity | LiveKit SIP + Twilio Elastic SIP trunk | Twilio Media Streams over WebSocket; Telnyx | LiveKit handles SIP, jitter and codecs natively. The call is just a room participant, so the same agent code works for local testing and real calls. Twilio is the best-documented trunk; Telnyx is cheaper but adds setup risk for a 6-hour project. |
| STT | Deepgram Nova-3 (streaming) | Whisper / gpt-4o-transcribe, AssemblyAI | Lowest streaming latency of the options I've used, robust on 8 kHz audio, and keyterm boosting for the patient's name. Non-streaming Whisper would add a second or more per turn. |
| LLM | GPT-4.1-mini | GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet, Llama on Groq | Short in-character replies don't need a large model. Time-to-first-token matters more. Groq would be faster still, but tool-calling reliability (`end_call`, `hold_silently`) mattered more. |
| TTS | Cartesia Sonic-3 | ElevenLabs Flash, OpenAI TTS | Fast first audio, natural voice, cheap. ElevenLabs is comparable in quality; OpenAI TTS was noticeably slower to first byte. |
| Turn detection | Silero VAD + LiveKit semantic turn detector, 0.7 s min delay | VAD-only silence threshold; STT endpointing | Pure silence thresholds either interrupt mid-thought pauses or feel sluggish everywhere. The semantic model can wait longer only when the sentence sounds unfinished. |
| Interruptions | The clinic may interrupt the bot (min 0.6 s of speech, false-interruption resume). A scripted barge-in is used in one scenario. | Disable interruptions | Realistic behaviour, and it lets us test the clinic's own barge-in handling deliberately rather than by accident. |
| Recording | LiveKit session recorder (stereo, both sides on one timeline) | LiveKit Egress to S3; Twilio call recording | No extra storage or credentials, it stays in sync with our turn timestamps, and per-side channels make analysis easy. |
| Excluded by the brief | No realtime or speech-to-speech models, no hosted voice-agent platforms | | Required by the challenge. A pipeline is also easier to inspect and tune per stage. |
